## 1.1 Why not 3 — and where the real floor is

B3 was sealed and trained, and **failed on the first iteration where the
Gaussian blur was drawn**:

    RuntimeError: Padding size should be less than the corresponding input
    dimension, but got: padding (3, 3) at dimension 1 of input [1, 3, 128, 128]

**The limit is not architectural, it is in the augmentation pipeline.** The
`default` preset includes `GaussianBlurTransform` with `blur_sigma=(0.3, 1.5)`
and `truncate=6`. The kernel is `round_to_odd(sigma·6 + 0.5)` and the padding is
`ksize // 2`, applied with `mode="reflect"` — which in PyTorch requires padding
**strictly smaller** than the dimension:

| sigma | ksize | padding | minimum depth |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.3–0.5 | 3 | 1 | 2 |
| 0.8 | 5 | 2 | 3 |
| 1.0–1.2 | 7 | 3 | 4 |
| **1.4–1.5** | **9** | **4** | **5** |

**The practical floor of this axis is 5 slices.** At 5, the maximum padding of 4
passes. At 3, any sigma ≥ 1.0 breaks it — and B3 drew one.

**Technical note that follows:** with `augmentation_preset: default`, the
minimum depth in z is 5 slices. The failure only surfaces when the blur is
drawn, which can take minutes or hours of training.

---

**5 slices (48.0 µm) is the floor of the axis**, for the reason above. Not an
arbitrary choice: it is the smallest value the official recipe admits without
changing the augmentation preset, which would be a second variable.

- **5 indistinguishable** → *from 48.0 to 163.1 µm, a factor of 3.4 in depth,
  the model reads the same thing*. Closes the axis and raises a better question:
  what is the window doing at all.
- **5 breaks** → the collapse sits between 5 and 9, and 7 would localise it.

    H_floor:    the stroke information is in the central slices; 5 ~ 17
    H_collapse: below 86.4 µm the stem loses necessary context; 17 > 5

## 4. Windows — the same 60, third pass

New A/B assignment, seed 202608185.

**Declared risk:** the judge has seen these 60 windows twice. Memory of the
content is likely. The A/B assignment is redrawn and he never knew which side
was which. Accepted limit.

**Judging criterion, explicit** (correcting the under-specification recorded in
the ACC1 erratum):

- **counts as a win:** a letter separated from its neighbours on one side and
  not the other; a letter identifiable on one side and ambiguous on the other; a
  stroke whole on one side and broken on the other
- **does NOT count, it is a tie:** a difference in contrast, smoothness or
  rounding without a difference in shape or integrity; no identifiable letter on
  either side
- **does NOT count as readability:** a very straight stroke aligned with its
  neighbours, suspected fibre (`err` and `Blue`, 2026-08-16)

## 6. Confounders

Inherited, plus:

**6.1 — Window memory**, third pass (§4).

**6.2 — The jitter dominates.** With 5 slices, `max_offset: 2` shifts the window
by up to **40%** of its own width, against 12% at 17. The augmentation stops
being a light perturbation and starts moving a relevant part of the content.
**Serious confounder:** if b05 breaks, it could be from lack of window **or**
from excessive jitter, and this design does not separate them.

Holding the jitter fixed is the right call — changing it would introduce a
second variable — but the limit is real. **If b05 breaks, the next experiment is
b05 with `max_offset: 0`, to separate the causes.**

**6.3 — The blur at the limit.** At 5 slices the maximum blur padding is 4
against a dimension of 5 (§1.1). It passes, but by one unit. Any change to the
augmentation preset breaks this arm.
