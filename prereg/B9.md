## 1. Why this point

B13 established that **13 slices (124.7 µm) are indistinguishable from 17**:
60 ties out of 60 under blind judgement, with demonstrably distinct models
(r 0.74–0.95, median |d| 9.04 levels).

One point says nothing about where the limit is. With **9 slices (86.4 µm)** the
axis gets a third point, and both readings are useful:

- **9 also indistinguishable** → the window can be cut **in half** at no cost to
  readability. A far stronger claim than B13 alone.
- **9 breaks** → the limit sits between 9 and 13, and there is an identifiable
  optimum. More interesting still.

**No outcome is uninformative.**

    H_half:  the window can drop to 86.4 µm with no loss; 9 ~ 17
    H_limit: below 124.7 µm the model loses necessary context; 17 > 9

measurements against the same reference.

Estimated cost: ≤ 6 h (b13 ran at 3.70 it/s against 3.24 for m17).

## 4. Windows — the same 60 as B13

**The same 60 window positions**, with a **new A/B assignment** drawn with seed
202608179. This allows the direct claim *"across the same 60 windows, 9, 13 and
17 are indistinguishable"*. Drawing new windows would add sample variation
between the two comparisons for no gain.

**Declared risk:** the judge has seen these 60 windows once. Partial mitigation:
the A/B assignment is redrawn, and he never knew which side was which — there is
no assignment information to remember. What can persist is memory of the
*window*, not of the *verdict per arm*. Accepted limit.

## 6. Confounders

Inherited from B13 §7, plus:

**6.1 — Window memory** (§4).

**6.2 — The jitter shifts proportionally more.** With 9 slices, ±2 is 22% of the
window against 12% at 17. The augmentation is relatively stronger in the narrow
arm. That is an unavoidable consequence of holding the jitter fixed, and
changing it would introduce a second variable. Declared.
